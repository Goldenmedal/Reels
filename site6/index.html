On being a gay man

A short essay on growing up, finding your people, and the long road to being yourself.

For most of the last century, being a gay man was treated as a crime. England and Wales decriminalized sex between men in 1967. For many people, that change felt like the first crack of light under a very heavy door.

Change in medicine came slowly. The World Health Organization followed in 1999, removing homosexuality from the International Classification of Diseases. For the men who had spent years being told something was wrong with them, the feeling of finally being seen is something many still talk about.

A different kind of story

Every gay man has his own version of growing up. For some it is quiet and slow, a feeling that takes years to put into words. For others it arrives all at once, in a single conversation they still remember perfectly.

What most of these stories share is a moment of recognition: the point where a vague sense of being different turns into something with a name, and then into something that feels like home.

Finding your people

Friendship sits at the center of it. The group chat, the friend who gets it without explanation, the dinner that runs three hours too long. These are the places where people stop performing and start belonging.

Community looks different for everyone. It might be a club, a sports team, a group of coworkers or just two people on a couch. What matters is that it feels safe enough to be fully yourself.

What still matters

There is still work to do, and plenty of people are still figuring things out. But the conversation keeps changing every time someone decides to be honest about who they are.

If you are somewhere in the middle of that story right now, take your time. There is no deadline, and there is no wrong way to arrive.
